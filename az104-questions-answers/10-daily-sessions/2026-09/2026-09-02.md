## Key Concepts Learned

### Managed Identity

System-assigned:

- Tied to the Azure resource lifecycle
- Appropriate when the resource needs its own identity
- Identity is deleted with the resource

User-assigned:

- Independent Azure resource
- Can be assigned to multiple resources
- Survives replacement of an individual workload
- Useful when multiple workloads share an identity

---

## Authentication vs Authorization

Authentication:

Who are you?

Authorization:

What are you allowed to do?

Important troubleshooting pattern:

Token acquisition succeeds
    +
HTTP 403
    ->
Investigate authorization and service access controls

## RBAC Scope

Management Group
    ->
Subscription
    ->
Resource Group
    ->
Resource

Parent-scope assignments can be inherited by child resources.

Least privilege means selecting the smallest scope that satisfies the requirement.

---

## Important Permission

Microsoft.Authorization/roleAssignments/write

This permission is required to create an Azure RBAC role assignment.

Contributor does not provide this permission.

## Troubleshooting Pattern

For Azure access failures:

1. Identify the principal.
2. Determine whether authentication succeeded.
3. Identify the attempted operation.
4. Determine management plane vs data plane.
5. Identify the required role or permission.
6. Verify assignment scope.
7. Check inheritance.
8. Check service-specific access controls.
9. Check network restrictions when relevant.
10. Apply least privilege.
